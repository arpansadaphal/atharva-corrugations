## 1. Executive summary

**What this company is, on the evidence.** One manufacturing plant at Ranjangaon MIDC, Pune, operating since 2014, part of the Atharva Group. It makes standard and custom corrugated boxes, die-cut and heavy-duty packs, sheets, pads, partitions, inserts, rolls, and a set of allied protective products (angle corners, honeycomb, paper cores, pulp trays, tape). It offers in-house packaging design from drawings, CAD files or a physical product, and says it tests in-house. [Fact/Verify]

**The strategic reading.** Corrugated packaging is bought on specification, evidence and responsiveness, repeatedly, by people who compare three or four suppliers on the same spec sheet. The website should therefore work as a **"specify → quote" instrument**, not a brochure: it must let a buyer confirm fit in minutes, see proof that is specific and consistent, and send a usable quotation request.

**Seven recommendations**

1. **Make the quote request the primary conversion, with WhatsApp always reachable beside it.** A structured request captures what a supplier needs to price a box; WhatsApp serves fast, small and drawing-led enquiries. (§5)
2. **Gate every claim on evidence.** The client has ticked broadly and supplied little proof. A claims registry that blocks unverified statements at build time keeps the site honest and makes launch a checklist, not a debate. (§6; Technical §4.3)
3. **Lead with design-to-delivery, and with board-making if confirmed.** In-house structural design is confirmed. If the plant runs its own corrugator (strongly indicated, unconfirmed: C-08), "we make the board and the box" becomes the headline difference from converters. (§4)
4. **Be candid about what is made at the plant and what is arranged with partners.** Varnish/coating is outsourced; several allied products are unverified. Transparency is the cheapest credibility available. (§4, §6)
5. **Keep the site lean: one hub, five product families, one industries page, one custom-packaging page, one manufacturing-and-quality page.** No metrics section, no export section, no case studies, no gallery page, no sustainability page at launch, because the evidence does not exist yet. (Sitemap §1)
6. **Show the Atharva Group clearly but modestly**: a name and the Group office, not claims the client has not supplied. (§8)
7. **Measure from day one**, including a source token in WhatsApp messages, and promise no lead volumes. (§5.6)

**What blocks launch** (full list in Audit §7): domain and DNS control (C-02), ISO certificate, machinery list and corrugator confirmation, photographs, logo and brand colours, privacy policy.

**Where this plan is thin:** the competitor sample is small, no keyword-volume data was available, and there are no brand assets yet. Each is called out where it matters.

## 3. Audience and buying journey

### 3.1 Audience (client-confirmed customer types: auto OEMs and suppliers, industrial manufacturers, FMCG, food, pharma, e-commerce sellers, exporters)

| Buyer | Goal | Typical questions | What the site must show |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Procurement manager** (OEM, industrial) | A short list of qualified suppliers; low risk | Is this a real plant? Certified? Will they respond? Can customers audit them? | ISO (once verified), plant evidence, process, customer-audited statement, quote form, reply time |
| **Packaging / design engineer** (OEM, NPD, QA) | A pack that protects the product at the right cost | Which board, flute, GSM? Can they design from my product? Can they test? | Spec panels, flute guide, design-to-delivery steps, test practices, sample request |
| **Plant, logistics or operations manager** | Fewer damages, efficient pallets, dependable supply | Heavy-duty options? Edge protection? Moisture? Delivery rhythm? | Heavy-duty and protective families, handling notes, supply information (Q-24, gated) |
| **E-commerce / FMCG buyer (SME)** | Quick price for a defined box | Sizes? Printing? Small runs? | WhatsApp, short quote path, standard box styles |
| **Exporter's packaging lead** | Export-grade packs | Moisture resistance? Strength? Pallets? | Heavy-duty and export-type packs described as **product types only**; no export claims |

### 3.2 Journey and where the website acts

Discover (search, marketplace, referral) → **Evaluate** ("do they make what I need, and can they prove it?") → **Specify** (dimensions, board, print, quantity) → **Request quote** → Sample and approval → Supply. The site covers the first four steps and starts the fifth (sample request).

### 3.3 What a buyer needs before contacting, and where it is answered

| Buyer question | Where answered | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|
| Do you make my kind of box? | Product families, formats | Fact (formats) |
| Which board can you run? | Family spec panels, flute guide | Verify |
| Can you design it from my product or drawing? | Custom Packaging | Fact |
| What do you test? | Manufacturing & Quality | Verify |
| Are you certified? | Certificate card | Verify |
| What is made at your plant? | Capability matrix | Verify (C-07) |
| How do I request a quote and what do you need? | Quote page, "what we need" lists | Fact |
| How fast will you reply? | Quote page, Contact | Fact (2 working days) |
| Who owns the die and plates? | Custom Packaging FAQ | Missing (Q-23) |
| How do I reach a person? | Phones, WhatsApp, hours | Fact |

Specific, evidence-based, operational; written like an experienced manufacturing partner. **Banned:** world-class, industry-leading, cutting-edge, revolutionary, next-generation, global presence, "trusted partner delivering excellence", and any superlative without proof. **CTA vocabulary:** Request a quote · Chat on WhatsApp · Send us your drawing · Request a sample. **Never:** Get started, Book a demo, Schedule a consultation.

**Claims the site will not make:** exports or countries (C-01); capacity, area, team size (C-10); food or pharma compliance; FSC/PEFC; WRAP; named customers; lead times; MOQ; revenue; "largest", "leading", "best".

## 5. Conversion strategy

### 5.1 Primary conversion: Request a Quote

Why: it is goal #1 and #2 (quotations, OEMs); OEM procurement runs on formal requests; a structured request yields specification-complete leads and shortens the first reply. A bare "call us" produces vague leads.

### 5.2 Secondary conversions

| Action | Role | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **WhatsApp** | Co-equal fast path; the client lists it first | Always visible (floating button, hero, contact, quote band). Pre-filled message names the page or product and carries a source token |
| Call | Immediate contact | Click-to-call on mobile; hours stated |
| Sample request | A type of quote request | For engineers evaluating a design |
| Product enquiry | Pre-filled quote request from a family page | Family and format pre-selected |
| Career enquiry | Separate path to HR | Not mixed into the sales form |
| General enquiry | Short contact form | Routes per F-13 |

**Hierarchy [Proposal, SD-02]:** the quote button carries the primary visual weight; WhatsApp is never hidden or demoted. The client lists WhatsApp first; the plan keeps both prominent and lets the data decide what to emphasise after launch.

### 5.3 What the buyer sees before asking for a quote

Product fit and formats; board options; the design-to-delivery path; test practices; certificate; what to send; reply time. **No** price ranges, MOQ or lead times (not supplied; MOQ withheld).

### 5.4 Quote-request philosophy

Ask only what changes the price or feasibility. Required: who you are, what you pack, roughly how many. Optional but encouraged: dimensions, board, print, delivery place, date. Dimensions are **internal** length × width × height in millimetres, with a small diagram to prevent the common internal/external mix-up (buyer guidance, E-07). Allow more than one quantity so buyers can compare price breaks. No file upload at launch; drawings come by WhatsApp or by replying to the confirmation email (Sitemap §7, Technical §7).

### 5.5 Response commitment

The client states a 2-working-day reply. The site says so plainly and sets a separate expectation for WhatsApp (business hours). The commitment is only as good as the inbox: see risk S-04 and Roadmap post-launch review.

### 5.6 Success definition and measurement

Recommended outcomes (no volume promised; targets are set after 90 days of data):

| KPI | Definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Qualified quote requests** | Submitted via the quote page with product, quantity and at least two spec fields, or a WhatsApp thread tagged qualified by sales | Form events; sales tag |
| Spec completeness | Share of requests with dimensions and board filled | Form data |
| Segment mix | Share of requests from target industries and from OEM-type companies | Industry field; sales tag |
| Custom-packaging requests | Requests flagged die-cut, drawing-led or sample | Form data |
| WhatsApp starts, call taps | Click events by location (nav, hero, floating, band) | Analytics |
| First-response time | Median time from request to first reply | Inbox / sales log |
| Sales conversations generated | Requests that became a quotation or a call | Monthly 15-minute review with sales |

Because analytics cannot see what happens inside WhatsApp, the pre-filled message includes a short source tag (for example "Web: die-cut page") that sales can read and log.

## 7. Competitive and buyer-expectation research

### 7.1 Method and limits

Read on 12 Oct 2026: one local WordPress site; three local marketplace profiles; one national manufacturer site; one Maharashtra manufacturer's marketplace mini-site; four international custom-corrugated specialists' service pages; five buyer and standards guides on quotation inputs and flute selection. **Not done:** keyword-volume or traffic tools, usability testing, competitor analytics. The sample is small; findings are patterns, not a market study. Most directly comparable local rivals (same MIDC and nearby Chakan) were reachable mainly through marketplace profiles.

### 7.2 Observed sites

| Site | Type | What it does well | Gaps and cautions | Adopt / adapt / avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **PackSquare** (Alandi / Chakan, Pune) · packsquare.in | Local manufacturer, WordPress | States installed capacity, shop-floor size, annual output and named machinery; separate pages by ply; WhatsApp widget; founder profiles; hours | Generic "benefits of corrugated" copy; location-stuffed page names; no specification tables; contact route is a link, not a structured request | **Adopt** specific plant facts (once the client releases them). **Adapt** ply-level information as sections, not thin pages. **Avoid** keyword-stuffed URLs and generic copy |
| **Paack In India** (D-75, Ranjangaon MIDC) | Closest neighbour; marketplace profile only | Heavy-duty focus; lists sectors; mentions a quality lab | No site found; experience claimed as 20 years on one listing and 40 on another | **Opportunity:** a consistent, specific site is a differentiator. **Avoid** inconsistent claims |
| **Spruce Pax, Tayal Packaging** (Chakan, Pune) | Marketplace-first sellers | Product names by ply and style; ratings; price ranges | Buyers compare them on marketplaces; no engineering evidence | **Adapt** ply and style vocabulary for search match |
| **TGI Packaging** (Chennai) · tgipackaging.in | Large national manufacturer, WordPress | Clear sections: overview, milestones, infrastructure, manufacturing process, awards, products, customers, careers, contact. Publishes capability tiles, including CNC sample making, own fleet, ERP | Hero slider; counters; the same page quotes **different tonnages** (33,000 and 50,000 t/yr) and an experience figure that disagrees with its incorporation year | **Adapt** the manufacturing-process and capability structure. **Avoid** unmaintained numbers: every figure comes from one registry (Technical §4.3) |
| **Diptic Corrugating** (Shahapur) · marketplace mini-site | Maharashtra manufacturer | States corrugator width, ply range (3 to 13), lab, industries | Marketplace formatting; no RFQ structure | **Adapt** the habit of stating machine size and ply range plainly |
| **Atlantic Packaging, Crown Packaging, Green Bay Packaging** (US) | Custom-corrugated specialists | Frame capability as **design → test → die-cut → supply**; name the standards they test to; CAD table and sample making; "request a sample" | US context; they hold standards (ISTA, ASTM) Atharva has not claimed | **Adopt** the structure. **Avoid** naming any standard or test the client has not confirmed |
| **MDI, Shorr, CMI** (US) | Custom packaging | A five-step "pick product → colour → dimensions → features → quote" path; design-and-test narrative | Consumer-brand language ("unboxing") irrelevant here | **Adapt** the guided quote idea as a short, single-page form |

### 7.3 Findings that shape the plan

1. **The local market is marketplace-first.** Buyers meet suppliers on IndiaMART-style profiles that show product names and prices but little engineering. A specific, consistent, spec-grade site stands apart.
2. **Specifics are the baseline.** A small Pune-area rival publishes capacity, floor area and machinery; a large national one publishes tonnage, infrastructure and fleet. Withholding area, capacity and team (C-10) makes Atharva look thinner than it may be. If the plant really exceeds 100,000 sq ft, hiding it undersells the company.
3. **Inconsistent numbers destroy trust.** One page quoting two tonnages is a lesson in single-source-of-truth data.
4. **No observed local site shows specification tables, test evidence or a structured quote request.** That is the opening.
5. **Specialists structure capability as design, test, convert, supply**, which matches Atharva's confirmed design-support offer.
6. **Buyer guidance converges on the same inputs** for a fair quote: internal dimensions, board grade and flute, strength requirement, print, several quantities, delivery place, in-hand date, and who owns dies and plates. These become the quote fields and an FAQ.
7. **WhatsApp widgets are common locally; a product-aware pre-filled message with a source token was not observed.** A small, cheap differentiator.

### 7.4 What this does not tell us

Which keywords have volume; how buyers behave on mobile; what Atharva's actual prospects use to find suppliers. Search Console data after launch is the first real evidence (Roadmap §8).

## 8. Atharva Group strategy

- **Presence.** The client wants the Group mentioned prominently. Recommended: an "An Atharva Group company" line next to the logo in the header, a Group panel in the footer with the Group office (F-08), a short Group section on About. Not a banner, and not Group claims the client has not supplied.
- **No sister-company list or cross-links** until the client supplies the canonical list, descriptions and approvals (C-18, Q-26).
- **One Group fact source.** Group name, Group office, founders' names and any approved company list live in one shared constants module so five sites cannot drift (Technical §4.3, §14).
- **No cross-contamination.** Marketplace text already attributes Group customers to this company (C-15). The build carries a banned-string check (Technical §13).
- **Independence.** Hosting, repository and domains are not assumed shared (Technical §3, §12).
- **Family resemblance without cloning.** Shared engineering, type, spacing, motion principles, accessibility; company-specific colour, hero, graphic device and photography (Design §1, §15).
